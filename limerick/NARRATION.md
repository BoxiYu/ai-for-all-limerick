## intro
A winter's night in Limerick. Behind these windows, people are doing ordinary things: cooking, learning, remembering, keeping in touch. And quietly, AI is helping. Let's look inside.

## kitchen
Six o'clock. Two leeks, a few eggs, a bit of cheese. Mary takes a photo of the fridge and asks what she could make tonight. It suggests a few ideas, and she chooses. She knows her own kitchen.

## nature
Seven o'clock. A robin is singing by the porch lamp, the way robins do on Irish winter nights. Tom opens the window and holds up his phone. A free app listens, and tells him the name of the bird.

## library
Half seven, at the library. Anne is learning something new. She asks a question, then asks again, as many times as she needs. It never sighs. It sounds very sure of itself, and it isn't always right, so for the things that matter, she checks.

## travel
Eight o'clock. John and Kathleen are planning a trip to Italy, and it helps them sketch out the days. When they get there, a phone held over the menu turns the Italian into English. They downloaded the language before they left.

## photos
Half eight, by the fire. Thousands of photos on one phone. He types: dog on the beach. And there she is, years ago, running into the waves. His mother laughs before he can say a word.

## card
Nine o'clock. A card has come from her granddaughter, and her eyes are not what they were. She takes a photo of it, and a free app reads the words aloud and describes the picture: a robin on a holly branch. She doesn't have to wait for anyone.

## verse
Half nine. A card to write to a daughter in Australia, and the words won't come. She asks for a few ideas for a short verse, then changes it until it sounds like her. The last line, she writes herself. That one is the best.

## weather
Ten o'clock, the forecast. Behind it, Europe's weather scientists now run an AI forecast alongside their usual one, and people are still in charge. Tomorrow, the forecaster says, looks bright. The wellies are already out.

## journey0
One last look. When Mary asked about her fridge, where did her question go?

## journey1
It left her phone as a radio signal and reached a phone mast. From there it travelled as pulses of light, through fibre-optic cables under streets and fields.

## journey2
It arrived at a data centre: big, quiet buildings full of computers, often far from where we live.

## journey3
Inside are special chips. Each one does a huge number of small sums, all at the same time. That is how it works out an answer in a second or two.

## journey4
All of it runs on electricity, from wind farms and power stations, through the grid. Data centres use a large share of Ireland's electricity. That is a real cost, and it's worth knowing.

## journey5
And then the answer comes all the way back, to a kitchen in Limerick. Leek and potato soup.

## finale
Eight windows, one winter's night. None of it is magic, and none of it replaces us. It helps us cook, learn, remember, and stay close. Let it help. Keep the judgement.
